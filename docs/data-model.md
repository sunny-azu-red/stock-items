# Data model

The schema is created by [`internal/db/migrations`](../internal/db/migrations): `00001_init.sql` holds all of it, and later migrations change it from there. Migrations are applied automatically when the app starts. Goose records them in `goose_db_version`.

```
vehicles ─┬─< item_vehicles >─┬─ items ─┬─< item_skus
          │                   │         ├── item_photos (1:1)
          │                   │         ├─< stock_movements >── service_records
          ├─< service_kits ─< service_kit_lines >─┘                   │
          └─< service_records ────────────────────────────────────────┘
items ─< shopping_entries (or free text)
trash (JSON snapshots of deleted rows; no foreign keys)
form_submissions (one-time form keys; no foreign keys)
```

## Tables

**Column order**: every table has `id` first, then `version`, then its own columns, then `created_at`, `updated_at`, `archived_at`, `deleted_at` (whichever it has). A migration that adds a column puts it in its place with `reorder_columns`; see [conventions](conventions.md#database).

**vehicles:** name (unique, case-insensitive), kind (`motorcycle`/`car`/`other`), make, model, year, plate, VIN, chip colour, `odometer`, `purchased_on` (date) and `odometer_at_purchase` (the reading it was bought at), all three `NOT NULL` since 00004 as the schedules need them (make, model, year, plate and VIN are only shown, and optional), optional `first_registered_on` (00008: the delivery certificate's Registration date, `CHECK (first_registered_on <= purchased_on)`; empty for a vehicle bought new), optional `warranty_ends_on` (the warranty's last day) and `warranty_ends_at` (the reading it ends past; `> 0`), notes, `archived_at`. Distances (`odometer`, `odometer_at_purchase`, a service's `odometer`, a kit's `interval_distance`) are in the unit `APP_DISTANCE` names (km by default); it is only a label, nothing is converted.
- When a service kit has an interval but has never had a service performed (`LastService` is nil), reminders count from the manual's milestones (`stock.Start`, `dueFrom` in `stock/kits.go`): from 0 and `first_registered_on` (`purchased_on` when empty), the interval's last milestone at or before the purchase (`odometer_at_purchase`, `purchased_on`), so the first due is the next one after it, as if the first owner had kept to the manual. A vehicle bought new at 0 counts from its purchase as before. Logging a service supersedes the milestones for that kit.
- A warranty runs until `warranty_ends_on` has passed or the odometer is above `warranty_ends_at`, whichever comes first (`stock.WarrantyOf`, worked out on the app's day). It ends soon in its last `DueSoonDays` (30) days or `WarrantySoonDistance` (1 000) of distance. Neither end is checked against `purchased_on` or `odometer_at_purchase`: a vehicle bought used may have been bought after its warranty ended, or past its reading. The vehicle form's Has a warranty box (ticked also for one that has ended) needs `warranty_ends_on` (the reading is optional); unticked, the handler saves neither.
- The odometer can't be set below `odometer_at_purchase`, and a service can't be dated before `purchased_on` or read below `odometer_at_purchase` (`stock.VehicleInput.Validate`, `checkServiceOdometer`); a purchase date or reading can't be set after or above a service already logged (`checkVehiclePurchasedOn`, `checkVehicleOdometerAtPurchase`). Deleting a service never takes the odometer below `odometer_at_purchase` (`restoreOdometer`).

**items:** name, brand, category, `unit` (`piece`/`ml`/`g`), `quantity`, `min_quantity` (low-stock threshold), `expires_on`, location, notes.
- `quantity >= 0` is enforced by a check constraint.
- Quantities are always in the base unit: 1.5 L is stored as `1500`.
- **Amounts keep at most two decimals of the base unit**: `items.quantity` and `min_quantity`, `item_skus.pack_size`, `service_kit_lines.quantity`, `stock_movements.delta` and `shopping_entries.quantity` are `numeric(12,2)`. Pieces are whole. `stock.ParseAmount` refuses finer input (`0.234 g`, `1.234567 kg`, `1.0004` pieces) instead of rounding it; amounts the code computes (differences, sums) go through `stock.RoundQty`.
- `archived_at` is set when an item that other records refer to is archived instead of deleted (see below).
- `expires_on` is set on the item form, or replaced by a purchase that gives the new batch's best-before date (in the same transaction as the movement).

**item_skus:** many part numbers per item: `code`, `kind` (`oem`/`manufacturer`/`barcode`/`retailer`/`other`; `barcode` codes must be a GTIN-8/12/13/14 with a valid check digit), `brand` (e.g. "KTM", "Motorex"), `pack_size` (base unit). Codes are unique per item.

**item_vehicles:** many-to-many tags.
- A vehicle with services or kits can't be deleted (`store.DeleteVehicle` returns a `ConflictError`); it is archived instead (`ArchiveVehicle`): it leaves the vehicle lists, pickers, filters and the sidebar, and its kits leave reminders and suggestions (`ListKits` skips them unless asked by id or vehicle), but its services, kits, labour costs and item tags stay. It takes no new services or kits (`checkActiveVehicle`), and no kit moves to it (`UpdateKit`); restoring a deleted past service or kit of it is allowed, as that isn't new work. `ListVehicles` returns archived vehicles too, flagged, for chips and lookups. A vehicle without either is deleted, which only removes its tags.
- An item with no tags is "General" stock.

**Deleting an item:** the foreign keys on `item_id` cascade, so a delete would also remove its movements, kit lines and shopping entry. `store.DeleteItem` therefore refuses an item with purchases, service movements or kit lines (`store.ItemUsage`) with a `ConflictError`. Such items are archived instead (`ArchiveItem` sets `archived_at` and moves its shopping entry to the trash). `ListItems` hides archived items unless `ItemFilter.Archived` asks for them; lookups by id (`GetItem`) always find them. Kit lines carry `Archived`: kit readiness, `SuggestPurchases`, the kit shortfall and a service started from the kit skip them. Archived items take no stock changes (nor edits of their quantity or history; `applyMovement`, `lockMovement` and restoring a stock change refuse them in the transaction). A service's own parts move with it: editing it may keep an archived part as it was (`checkArchivedParts`), and deleting or restoring it gives back or takes again what it used. Kit lines may name archived items (pickers just don't offer them). Archived items can't go on the shopping list (`AddShopping`/`EnsureShopping` return `ErrNotFound`), and the vehicles' item counts leave them out.

**item_photos:** one photo per item, stored as JPEG (`image` up to 1600 px, `thumb` 320 px square).

**stock_movements:** the ledger.
- `kind`: `starting` (stock already on the shelf when the item was created), `bought`, `used`, `adjusted` (an adjustment, an edit or a quick +/-), `service`.
- A new item's starting stock is `bought` when a price or shop is entered on the form (`stock.ItemInput.StartMovement`), otherwise `starting`. Starting stock can also carry an optional note (`stock.ItemInput.BoughtNote`). Only priced purchases feed the last price, stock value and spending.
- `delta`: signed, in base units, rounded to two decimals (`stock.RoundQty`) before it is applied, so the stored delta is exactly the change to the quantity.
- Optional `total_price` and `supplier` (purchases only: `stock.MovementInput.Validate`), `note`, `occurred_on`. `occurred_on` must be between 1990-01-01 and tomorrow (`stock.CheckEventDate`).
- `service_record_id` is set for parts used by a service, and only for them: a CHECK (`stock_movements_service_record_check`) holds `(kind = 'service') = (service_record_id IS NOT NULL)`, and `stock.MovementInput.Validate` says the same. Its foreign key is `ON DELETE RESTRICT`: a service with parts can't be deleted in the database, nor its vehicle (which cascades to its services), so a service's parts always point at it. The store returns the parts and deletes their movements before the service (`DeleteService`, `UpdateService`).

**service_kits:** a named list of parts for a recurring job on one vehicle, with optional `interval_distance` / `interval_months`, `intermediate` (one intermediate service halfway between two full ones: by distance on a kit with one, every half `interval_distance` (an even one) at `interval_months`; by time on a kit with `interval_months` only, every half of it (an even number); so it needs a distance or a time (a CHECK, since 00007), `KitInput.Validate`; `stock.Kit.InterStep`, `InterMonths`), `break_in_at` (below), `warranty_required` (the vehicle's warranty asks for its break-in and full services on time: one missed loses it, [kit-schedules.md](kit-schedules.md); since 00006), `notes` and `archived_at`. A kit with logged services can't be deleted (that would set their `kit_id` to NULL and lose the kit's history); it is archived instead, which takes it out of `ListKits` (unless `IncludeArchived`), reminders (`Kit.Due` is `none`) and suggestions. The Services page provides an archived kits view (`archived=1`), and vehicle pages list both active and archived kits.

**service_kit_lines:** item, needed quantity and `intermediate` (`false` for full parts, `true` for intermediate parts). Each item appears at most once per schedule tier of a kit (`UNIQUE (kit_id, item_id, intermediate)`; rows of the same item in one tier on the form are added up, at most `stock.MaxQuantity`), so the same oil item can be in both the full and intermediate part lists of one kit. An index on `item_id` serves "which kits use this item". Kit lines also carry the item's `archived_at` timestamp when the part is archived.

**service_records:** a performed job: date, odometer, title, notes, labour cost (NULL when there is none, never 0: `CHECK (labor_cost > 0)`; `ServiceInput.Normalize` turns a typed 0 into none), optional kit and `kit_tier` (`full`, `intermediate` or `break_in`: which of that kit's services it was; since 00004, replacing `kit_intermediate`). Every service has a reading (`odometer`, NOT NULL since 00005; `ServiceInput.Validate` requires it): the schedules count from it. A break-in is a new vehicle's first service: no schedule counts from it (`ListKits`' `LastFullService` is the last `full` one, and `Kit.counted` skips it), so they run from new (the milestones, `stock.Start`) as a manual's do. A kit has one break-in on a vehicle, its first service there, read at most 10% past `break_in_at` (`store.checkKitTier`; a partial unique index keeps it to one), and only a kit whose `break_in_at` is above the vehicle's `odometer_at_purchase` (a vehicle bought new, `stock.Kit.HasBreakIn`) has one. A kit's optional `break_in_at` is above its vehicle's `odometer_at_purchase` (`store.checkKitBreakIn` on a kit's save, which refuses a value it already holds too; `checkVehicleBreakIns` on the vehicle's), and is the reading it is due at (`stock.Kit.NextIsBreakIn`, `BreakInDue`: no service logged with the kit yet, the vehicle bought below it and not yet past the kit's first distance). `odometer_before` is the reading logging it moved the vehicle's odometer forward from (NULL when it didn't move it), so deleting or editing it can put it back (`restoreOdometer`). 00005 dropped `odometer_moved` (it was `odometer_before IS NOT NULL`) and `odometer_at_log` (the vehicle's reading when a service was logged without one).

**trash:** what was deleted and can be restored: `kind` (`service`, `kit`, `movement`, `shopping`, `item`, `vehicle`, `photo`, `bought`), a `label`, and `payload`, a JSON snapshot of every row the delete removed (`{"table": [rows…]}`; for `bought` the entry as it was plus the purchase's `movement_id`). Written in the same transaction as the delete (`store/trash.go`), kept until the trash is emptied or the entry is permanently deleted. `RestoreTrash` re-inserts the rows with their original ids (`jsonb_populate_recordset`) and redoes their stock effect under a lock, refusing to go below zero; a reference to a row that is itself in the trash refuses the restore until that one is restored (`needRow`: "Restore Item: X first"), so the order of restores never loses a link; references permanently deleted are dropped (tags, kit lines, a service's kit) or refuse the restore (a service's vehicle or parts). A restore locks what it needs (vehicle, kit, items, in the usual order) before looking for it, so one deleted meanwhile is reported as above rather than failing the insert. Only the snapshot's columns are inserted, so columns added later get their defaults. A restore reports how many links it dropped (`Restored.Dropped`). `PurgeTrash` also deletes the entries that need a purged item or vehicle (for an item: deleted stock changes, photos, list entries and services that used it; for a vehicle: its deleted services and kits), since they could never be restored. A GIN index on `payload` serves the containment lookups (`payload @> {"items": [{"id": 5}]}`).

**shopping_entries:** linked to an item (at most one entry per item) or free text, with an optional quantity and note. `unit` is the unit of a free-text entry's quantity (`stock.ParseAmountAnyUnit` picks it from the typed suffix); linked entries use the item's unit.
- Which vehicles an entry is for isn't stored: `ListShopping` reads the item's tags and the active vehicles whose active kits use it, and `stock.ShoppingEntry.For` decides from them (free text and General items are every vehicle's). An item has one entry, so a shared item two vehicles need is one line, and a retag or a kit change shows at once.
- Marking a linked entry bought records a purchase. If less was bought than the entry's quantity, the entry stays with the rest (`store.MarkBought`).
- A free-text entry can be linked to an existing item with the same name (`store.ItemsNamed`, `store.LinkShopping`): the item is restored if archived, and the entry is merged into the item's own entry if it has one.
- A free-text entry can become a new item: `store.CreateItemFromShopping` creates the item with its starting purchase and deletes the entry in one transaction.

**version** (on `items`, `vehicles`, `service_kits`, `service_records`, `stock_movements`, `shopping_entries`): counts the changes to what the record's edit form or page shows, starting at 1. Edit forms and record buttons post it back, and the store refuses a stale one with `StaleError`. It moves on with an edit, archive or restore, a purchase's best-before date on the item, and any change to a shopping entry's quantity or note; it doesn't with stock quantity or odometer changes. SKUs, tags, kit lines and a service's parts count as part of their parent. A row restored from the trash gets its snapshot's version plus one (a snapshot without one gets 1).

**form_submissions**: the one-time keys of forms that were saved. `key` is the form's random key (32 hex characters), `result_id` the record the save made or changed (the item for the item page's stock forms, the entry for an add to the shopping list, none for Bought), `created_at` when. Each create or edit claims its form's key as the first statement of its transaction (`store.inFormTx`): a key already there means the form was saved already, and nothing is done again. Undoing a Bought deletes its key, which the `bought` snapshot keeps as `form_key`, so its form isn't "saved already" afterwards. Claims delete keys older than a day (`created_at < now() - interval '1 day'`, an instant, so no time zone is involved), with `SKIP LOCKED` so they never wait for each other.

## Dates and times

- **Instants** (`created_at`, `updated_at`, `archived_at`, `trash.deleted_at`, `form_submissions.created_at`) are `timestamptz`: Postgres keeps them in UTC, and every session of the app is in UTC (`db.Open` sets `timezone`). They are shown in the app zone: pgx scans them into `time.Local`, which `main` sets from `TZ` (or the machine's zone). Trash snapshots write them with their offset (`to_jsonb`), so they restore as the same instant.
- **Calendar days** (`stock_movements.occurred_on`, `service_records.performed_on`, `items.expires_on`, `vehicles.first_registered_on`, `vehicles.purchased_on`, `vehicles.warranty_ends_on`) are `date`: the day itself, with no zone. The app sets them (`expires_on` may be empty); none has a database default (`current_date` would be UTC's day, and an insert that forgets one fails instead). Go holds a day as midnight UTC (`stock.DateOnly`), as pgx reads a `date`.
- **Which day it is** is decided only in Go, in the app zone: today for form defaults, date checks, "expiring", reminders and spending; "Deleted today". SQL never turns an instant into a day (`TestNoZoneDependentSQL`).

## Invariants

1. `items.quantity` always equals `sum(stock_movements.delta)` for the item. Every change goes through `store.applyMovement` inside a transaction, and `store_test.go` asserts this after each scenario. The exceptions change recorded movements together with their effect, so the sum still holds:
   - deleting or editing a service (5);
   - editing or deleting a single movement (`store.UpdateMovement`/`DeleteMovement`, see 6);
   - restoring from the trash (rows come back together with their quantity change), including undoing a Bought.
2. A movement can never make a quantity negative. You get `InsufficientStockError` instead, and the transaction rolls back. A service with one missing part logs nothing.
3. An item's unit cannot change once it has stock history, is used in a kit, or has a quantity on the shopping list, counting what is in the trash, which would come back in the old unit.
4. Logging a service moves the vehicle odometer forward. It never moves it backwards. Deleting a service that moved it (`odometer_before` set) puts it back to the highest of `odometer_before` and the vehicle's other services' readings, or to the purchase reading, unless the odometer was changed since. Services that didn't move it leave it alone. A vehicle's reading can't be changed to less than its highest service reading, and a service's reading must fit between the services logged on earlier and later days (`checkVehicleOdometer`, `checkServiceOdometer`; service changes hold the vehicle's lock, so two saved at once take turns), also when a deleted service is restored.
5. A `service` movement always belongs to a service, and no other movement does (the CHECK and the RESTRICT foreign key above). Deleting a service deletes its `service` movements and puts the quantities back in stock. Editing a service (`store.UpdateService`) does the same for its old parts, takes the new ones, and redoes its odometer change, in one transaction; its vehicle can't change.
6. A single movement other than `service` can be edited (amount, price, shop, date, note) or deleted; the item's quantity moves by the difference. The change is refused if the quantity would go below zero. An `adjusted` change is edited as a signed change (added or took away, and how much), and `starting` stock that gets a price or shop becomes `bought` (`stock.ApplyEdit`).
7. A form or button never acts on a record that changed or was deleted since it was shown: the record's `version` is compared under the row's lock.
8. A create or edit form is saved at most once (within a day): its key is claimed in the transaction that saves it, before anything else, and a second claim of the same key waits for the first and then does nothing.

## Derived values (not stored)

| Value | Computed in |
|---|---|
| Low stock | `quantity < min_quantity` (`stock.Item.IsLow`) |
| Stock level (the stock badge) | `stock.Item.LevelFor(need)`: out (nothing left), short (less than a service's rows ask for, rows of one item together), low, or ok |
| Expiring | stock left and `expires_on` past or within 60 days of the app's date (`stock.Item.ExpiryState`, `store.ItemFilter.Expiring` with `Today`) |
| Kit readiness and shortfall | `stock.Kit.Readiness` (`ReadinessAt` / `ActiveLines`), `KitLine.Short` |
| What a kit's missing parts have on the shopping list | `stock.KitLine.Listed` (the item's entry asks for at least what the line misses) and `stock.ToList` (the lines it doesn't cover: the count on a parts card's **Add 2 missing**), from the entries' amounts |
| Service due state (`none`/`ok`/`soon`/`overdue`) | `stock.Kit.Due` (`BreakInDue`, `FullDue`, `InterDue`; `NextIsBreakIn`, `NextIsIntermediate`): last service with the kit + interval, compared to the current odometer and today. When a kit has an intermediate service (`intermediate`), it comes a step after the latest service of either kind (`InterDue`): `InterStep` (half the distance) at `interval_months`, or, on a kit with a time only, `InterMonths` (half the months); never from the break-in, unless that is also the first full one (below), and once it has been done since the last full one (`InterSinceFull`, worked out in `ListKits`), the full one is next. While the vehicle is under warranty (`Kit.UnderWarranty`, from `warranty_ends_on`/`warranty_ends_at`, worked out for today, so the intermediate services done while it ran count the other way once it has ended), the full schedule counts its own interval from `LastFullService` or the milestones before the purchase (`stock.Start`), and comes first when the intermediate service wouldn't fall due before it in distance or date (`dueBefore`); with a warranty or without, the full one comes first in the last quarter of its interval, `soon` or `overdue` (`fullClose`). A lost warranty (`lost`: a kit with `warranty_required` missed its break-in or a full service by more than the grace, `stock.WarrantyKit`, loaded by `store.loadWarrantyKits`) counts as none. Once it has ended or been lost, or without one, services take turns, each a step after the one before (`FullDue`). While the break-in is waited for, it is next, due at `break_in_at` or the kit's months from new (`BreakInDue`), and the full service counts the kit's own interval from new in every mode; the kit's first service by `break_in_at` + 10% must be the break-in (`store.checkKitTier`). No schedule counts from a break-in, unless it was done in the first full service's last quarter by time (`stock.BreakInIsFull`): then the time counts from it and the distance from 0 (`Kit.breakInFull`). One missed (past `break_in_at` and its 10% grace, nothing logged) is due as the full service at its reading. The whole model, with what each form refuses: [kit-schedules.md](kit-schedules.md). The distance counts from the last service's reading. "Soon" means within 30 days or max(500, 10 % of the interval) in the distance unit, but never more than half the interval (for days as for distance); exactly at the due date or odometer target is still "soon", one day or one unit past it is "overdue". Month intervals end on the same day of the month, or the month's last day (31 Jan + 1 month = 28 Feb). History logged with the kit on another vehicle is ignored. |
| Purchase suggestions | `stock.SuggestPurchases`: below minimum or short for any kit, and not already on the list |
| Stock value | quantity × last purchase price per unit, for items that aren't archived (`store.GetVehicleStats`) |
| Purchase count (item page) | `store.CountPurchases` |
| Spent this year | priced purchases dated from 1 January of the app's year up to and including the app's date (`store.GetVehicleStats`); a purchase dated tomorrow counts from tomorrow, and the figure starts again from zero each 1 January |
