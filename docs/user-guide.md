# User guide

## First steps

1. **Add your vehicles** (Vehicles → Add vehicle). Give each a short name such as "KTM 390" and a colour. That name and colour are used everywhere. What the reminders count from is required: under *Odometer* the current reading, **Purchased on** and **Odometer at purchase (km)** (0 for a new vehicle): until a kit's first service is logged, its reminders count from when you got the vehicle. The vehicle page shows them as *Purchased*; its edit form has them under *Purchase*, without the odometer, which you update on the vehicle's page (below). **Make**, **Model**, **Year**, **Plate** and **VIN** are only shown, so they are optional (a pedal bike has no VIN; with no odometer either, enter 0 and give its kits months only). The odometer can't be lower than the purchase reading, and no service can be dated before the purchase or read below it. Under *Warranty*, tick **Has a warranty** to enter when it ends (also one that has ended, e.g. before you bought the vehicle): **Warranty ends on** (its last day, required) and, if it has a limit, **Warranty ends at (km)** (a reading); whichever comes first ends it. Untick the box to remove it. The vehicle page shows the badge *Under warranty* beside its make and model, *Warranty ends soon* in its last 30 days or 1 000 km, then *Warranty ended*, and in red *Warranty lost* when a kit it asks for missed a service (below), and the date and reading as *Warranty ends* (*Warranty ended*). The vehicle's card on the Vehicles page and its row in the dashboard's Vehicles card say the same in words, and while it runs, its kits show *Warranty ends* next to their schedule. The warranty also decides how a kit's intermediate services count (Intermediate services, below). The Make and Model fields suggest makes and models you have already added.
2. **Add your items** (Add item, or press `a`):
   - **Name and brand.** Pick or type the manufacturer or factory brand (e.g. "KTM", "Motorex").
   - **Vehicles.** Click every vehicle the part fits. Leave all of them unselected for general stock (cleaners, grease, tools).
   - **Counted in.** Pieces, volume (ml / L) or weight (g / kg). Choose once; it can't be changed after stock changes are recorded, or once the item is in a kit or on the shopping list with a quantity, also when those are in the trash.
   - **In stock.** The amount you have right now, e.g. `525` ml or `1.5` L.
   - **Keep at least** (optional). Below this the item shows as *low* and is suggested on the shopping list.
   - **Purchase** (new items only, optional: bought it just now?). Enter the total price and/or the shop (picked from previously used shops or typed new), the date if it wasn't today, and an optional note (e.g. promo details or which SKU was bought). The starting stock is then recorded as a purchase, so the item shows a last price, a purchase count, and counts in the stock value and spending. Leave price and shop empty for stock you already had; it is recorded as *Starting stock* on the date given.
   - **SKUs.** Add every number you might search for: the OEM part number, the manufacturer number, barcode, one row per brand or pack size, each with the brand it's sold under (e.g. KTM for the OEM number, Motorex for the manufacturer's). They are listed with the item's own brand first, then OEM, manufacturer, retailer and other numbers, smaller packs first. A stock row shows the first two, and **+N more** shows the rest. Each one links to a web search for it (the search engine is set with `APP_SKU_SEARCH_URL`, see [deployment](deployment.md)).
   - Use **Save item and add another** to enter many items in a row. It keeps the vehicles and unit you picked.
   - **Duplicate** (on an item's page) starts a similar item with the same fields, SKUs and vehicles, but no stock, no best-before date and no barcodes, so scanning still finds the original. On a kit's page it starts a kit with the same name, schedule, parts and notes, for the vehicle you choose (the one you work on, if any), so a kit made for one bike is quick to set up for another; parts whose item is archived are left out.

## Daily use

- **Work on a vehicle.** With two or more vehicles, tap one under **Work on** in the sidebar (on a phone, under **More**), or **Work on …** at the top of its own page, to work on it: the dashboard, Stock, Services, Activity and Shopping then show that vehicle (with General items), and **Add item** starts with it ticked, while **Log service** and **Add kit** are for it without asking. A bar at the top of every page says **Working on …**, with the vehicle's warranty after its name (*· Under warranty*, *· Warranty ends soon* or *· Warranty ended*), and is the vehicle control meanwhile (the pages hide their vehicle chips; General items always come with it): tap another vehicle under **Work on** (on a phone, under **More**) to change, and the bar's **×** (*All vehicles*) stops and keeps your search. A search tells you when other vehicles have more (**N more in other vehicles**). While you work on one, the list pages always show it, even from an old bookmark, and links to other vehicles' lists are hidden; to see another vehicle, or to log a service or add a kit for it, work on it. Other vehicles' pages, kits and services, the vehicles list and Add vehicle wait until you switch or stop: an old link opens the vehicle you work on. Items and Trash always show everything. Meanwhile **Vehicle** in the sidebar opens its page; stop (the **×**) to list every vehicle or add one. It is remembered on each device, like the theme.
- **Dashboard.** Tiles for the number of items, low stock, expiring items, the **Shopping list**, the stock value (each item at its last purchase price; the tile opens what is in stock) and what you spent on items this year, since 1 January (priced purchases; the tile opens them in Activity; labour is on the Services page). The Low stock tile turns amber and the Expiring tile red while they have something in them, so a plain dashboard means nothing needs you. Then come upcoming services, recent activity, your vehicles (not while you work on one), **Low stock** (with how many items are suggested for the shopping list) and what is expiring. Each card links to its whole list (**All services →**, **All activity →**, …).
- **Stock page.** Click vehicle chips to filter (**All vehicles** shows every one). With two or more vehicles selected, choose *fits any* or *fits all*. **General** shows the items that fit no vehicle. On a row, click a vehicle chip or the category badge to show only those. Pick a **category**, or narrow it to **Low stock**, **Expiring**, **In stock only** or **Archived**; **Clear filters** resets. **Grouped** shows a section per vehicle set, like the copied text; **List** shows one table. Search matches name, brand, category, location, notes and any SKU code or brand, with or without dashes and spaces.
- **Sort by** (next to the stock toggles) orders the stock by **Name**, **Category**, **Location**, **Quantity**, **Best before** or **Recently changed**, and the buttons beside it flip the direction (*A–Z* / *Z–A*, *Low first* / *High first*, *Soonest first* / *Latest first*, *Oldest first* / *Newest first*). A new choice starts the way round that's usually wanted: names A to Z, the least first, the soonest best-before first, the most recently changed first. Items without a category, location or best-before date come last either way. Quantities are sorted per unit (grams, then ml, then pieces), as they don't compare. The grouped view sorts inside each section, and **Copy as text** and **CSV** come in the same order. The sort is part of the address; **Clear filters** keeps it, as it isn't a filter.
- **Getting around.** A page starts with its name. To go back, use the browser's Back (the mouse's back button, Alt+←, or a phone's back gesture), which also keeps the list's filters and place. The sidebar (on a phone, the tab bar) lights the list a page belongs to, one click away, and a service's or kit's vehicle chip under its name opens the vehicle.
- **Page buttons.** What you do on a page is at the top right of it (on a narrow screen, under its name). When they don't all fit, the least used (Delete or Archive, then Duplicate, then Edit) go into **⋯** at the end of the row; it opens a short menu of them.
- **Sortable tables.** On the Activity page, an item's history and a kit's parts, click a column header to sort by it, and click it again to reverse. The arrow shows the sorted column and its direction. Dates and amounts start with the newest or largest, text from A to Z. Activity sorts by date, item, type (*Type*), change or price (no price comes last either way); the type chips, the **Year** picker, **Older** / **Newer** and the CSV keep the sort, and a new sort starts again at the first page. A kit's parts sort by item, need, in stock or missing (what's missing first); custom sorting reveals **Reset order** to go back to the newest changes (on history and Activity) or to the kit's own order, the order its parts were added in (on a kit). Sorting an item's history keeps the page at the History card. Changes and kit amounts are sorted per unit, like the stock.
- **−/+ buttons** change stock by one step: 1 pcs, 100 ml or 50 g; + shows as *Adjusted* in the history, − as *Used*. For exact amounts, open the item:
  - **Bought**: amount, total price, shop, the date you bought it, best before and a note (the same form as **Bought** on the shopping list). Prices give you a stock value and spending totals. **Best before** replaces the item's date with the new batch's, so an expired item stops showing as expired. Leave it empty to keep the current date.
  - **Used**: take an amount from stock.
  - **Adjust**: set the stock to what is actually on the shelf; it shows as *Adjusted*.

  The history calls them *Bought*, *Used*, *Adjusted*, *Service* and *Starting stock* (what an item started with). Fields marked with a red star are required.
- **Fix a mistake in the history.** Every editable row in an item's history (and on the Activity page) has a pencil: change the amount, price, shop, date or note, or delete the entry. The stock moves by the difference, so a wrong price no longer sticks in the last price, stock value and spending. Purchases display their shop beside the type (*Bought · shop*), keeping the note column for your notes. Parts used by a service show *Used in this service* in the note column and have a **View service** button (an eye icon) that opens the service (fix them by editing the service itself). If the stock was used in the meantime, a change that would take away more than is left is refused; record the use or adjust the stock first. An adjustment (including quick +) is edited as a change: whether it added or took stock away, and by how much. Quick − is recorded as a use.
- **Copy as text** copies the current view as a plain list grouped by vehicle, handy for chats and notes. Where the browser won't copy (some do only over HTTPS), the text opens instead, to copy by hand. Ctrl-click (Cmd-click on a Mac) or middle-click opens the text in a new tab instead of copying it.
- **CSV** downloads the current view for a spreadsheet: quantities, SKUs (with brand and pack size), and for items bought with a price the last price (per piece, L or kg), the stock value at that price, and the date and shop of that purchase.
- **Activity** lists every stock change, newest first. Its chips narrow it to a vehicle (its items, shared ones too, and its own services' parts) and to **General** items, like the stock's; while you work on a vehicle, it shows that one with General.
- **Activity → CSV** downloads every stock change (or only the type and year you filtered on) with amounts, prices, shops and services, e.g. for your own spending overview.
- **Expiring** (dashboard tile and stock filter) lists items with stock left that are past or within 60 days of their best-before date. Used-up items don't show there.
- To edit an item's amount, type `0` if there is none left. A blank field is refused so the stock is never emptied by accident.
- Dates of purchases, stock changes and services can't be in the future (tomorrow is allowed for time zones) or before 1990. That keeps a year typo from becoming the last price or counting in the spending.

## Service kits and reminders

A **kit** is the parts list of a recurring job on one vehicle, e.g. *KTM 390 – Oil Service*: 1 pcs oil filter, 1.7 L oil, 2 pcs O-ring. Add one with **Add kit**, under the kits on the vehicle's page. The **Name** field suggests common maintenance jobs (*Oil Service*, *Air Filter*, *Brake Pads (Front)*, *Brake Fluid*, *Clutch Fluid*, *Coolant*, *Spark Plugs*, *Valve Check*, …) and learns any custom kit names or service titles you enter.

On kit and service part rows, type to find the item: the list shows the items that match with how much you have, only those for the form's vehicle: its own and shared ones (*For KTM 390*), then *General*. Pick the vehicle first (its chip); picking another one moves a row to that vehicle's item of the same name if it has one, else puts the row aside (it isn't saved), and picking the first one again brings it back. Shopping's Item lists items the same way on a vehicle's part of the list, and on the whole list as the stock page groups them (*Only KTM 390*, *Shared KTM 390 + KTM 1290*, *General*). Pick one (click it, or the arrow keys and Enter), or type its exact name (or brand and name, in any case); Enter with nothing highlighted just closes the list. A row keeps the item it has even if the item is renamed meanwhile. A part must be an item in stock for the vehicle, so a name that is no item, or one that two items share, or another vehicle's, is refused at its row when you save, with what you typed kept; archived items aren't offered, but a row that already has one keeps it. If you change an item's vehicles later, a logged service keeps it as it was, while a kit that uses it asks you to replace that part the next time you save the kit. Pick the unit next to the quantity: choosing an item offers its units and selects L or kg for liquids and weights, so `1.7` means 1.7 L (the grey hint is a number for that unit: *e.g. 1.5*). A unit typed into the quantity (`1700 ml`) wins. Each row shows the chosen item's stock as a badge:
- **Out of stock**, in red, when there is none.
- On a service, the amount in stock turns red, with an ✕, when the rows ask for more than there is. Rows of the same item count together. Saving would refuse it, so you see it before you save.
- Amber, with a ▲, when the item is below its minimum.
- Otherwise grey, for example **6 pcs in stock**.

A kit's rows never turn red for the amount: saving a kit takes nothing from stock.

- The kit page shows, per part, what you need, what you have and what is missing. Archived kit lines are marked so you can replace or remove them; they don't count as missing and aren't taken when you log the service.
- **Add 2 missing**, under a parts card, puts what that card's parts are missing on the shopping list; the number counts the parts not on the list yet. A row whose missing amount is on the list shows a cart instead of its red ✕, and once everything missing is listed the button's place shows **On shopping list**, which opens the list. A kit with an intermediate service has a card, and a button, for each schedule, and its header says which schedule its *Parts ready* or *1 part missing* is for (*Intermediate: 1 part missing*).
- Under **Schedule**, set a distance and/or months to get reminders on the dashboard: *On track*, *Due soon* or *Overdue*, counted from the last service (its date and odometer reading), or from the vehicle's purchase before the first one.
- **Intermediate service** (optional): once the kit has a distance (an even one, so it halves exactly), tick **Halfway between full services** for a lighter service halfway between two full ones (e.g. a DIY oil and filter change at 7 500 km between 15 000 km / 12-month dealer services), with its own parts list. It comes at half the distance, at the full schedule's months, whichever comes first, counted from the service before it. Once it has been done since the last full service, the full one is next. What it does to the full one depends on the vehicle's warranty (on the vehicle form): while the vehicle is **under warranty**, the intermediate service never pushes back the full service's deadline: it counts from the last full service, or from the purchase before the first one, and when it would come before the intermediate one, it takes over automatically. Once the warranty has ended (from the day after its last day, or past its reading; nothing to edit on the kit), or for a vehicle without one, nothing forces a full service: services take turns, each half the distance and the full months after the one before, and intermediate services done while it ran count that way too. With a warranty or without, once the full service is in the last quarter of its interval (2 500 km or about 3 months on 10 000 km / 12 months), it is next instead of an intermediate one you would skip anyway.
- **Break-in at (km)** (optional, once the kit has a distance): a new vehicle's first service, at the reading its manual says (e.g. at or before 1 000 km), with the full parts. It must be below the halfway reading when the kit has an intermediate service, and a break-in read more than 10% past it (1 100) is refused: log one done that late as **Full**. Past that reading with nothing logged, the kit asks for the Full in its place, overdue. It must be above the reading the vehicle was bought at: a vehicle bought used, past it, has no break-in here (Pitbox tracks the work done since you bought it), and its purchase reading can't be raised past a kit's Break-in at. Until something is logged with the kit, on a vehicle bought below that reading and not yet past the kit's distance, the kit reminds it (*Next: Break-in*) and **Log service** starts as **Break-in**. No schedule counts from it: the services after it are the distance and months after the purchase (the first full one at 8 000 km, not 9 000), as a manual and a warranty count them. The kit's first service on a vehicle, logged as **Full** before, can be marked **Break-in** on its edit form; a later one can't (one at most; only a kit with a **Break-in at** on a vehicle bought below it has one).
- **Required by the warranty** (Warranty section): tick it for a job the vehicle's warranty asks for on time, like the oil service. While the warranty runs, its break-in done more than 10% past Break-in at, or a full service more than 10% of its distance or 30 days past its limit (done late, or not done), loses the warranty: the vehicle shows *Warranty lost* in red, its page says what was missed, and its kits count as without a warranty. Intermediate services never count. Logging the missed services with their real dates and readings (for a kit added late) gets it back; otherwise it stays lost. The rules in full: [kit-schedules.md](kit-schedules.md).
- The kit page, dashboard and shopping list suggestions follow whichever schedule (*Full*, *Intermediate* or *Break-in*) is up next, and the kit page shows the full service's deadline next to an intermediate one.
- **Log service** on a kit's page opens the service form with the kit's parts filled in (for the schedule that is up next when the kit has an intermediate service or a break-in; you can also switch between **Full**, **Intermediate** and **Break-in** on the form, and the parts you changed for one come back when you switch back to it). The page's header names the vehicle and the kit. Check the schedule before saving: a service saved as **Full** restarts the full service's deadline. If the kit is deleted while the form is open, saving says so and the form goes on without the kit. Adjust quantities, remove what you didn't use, enter the odometer (required: the reminders count from it; it starts empty, with the vehicle's last reading shown in grey) and click **Save service**. Leave **Labour cost** empty for work you did yourself; 0 counts as empty. The parts are taken from stock and the vehicle's odometer is updated. If parts are missing from stock, every such row is marked and nothing is saved; **Add missing** then puts what's missing on the shopping list and leaves the form as you typed it.
- Made a mistake? **Edit** the service (on its page): change the date, odometer, work performed, labour, notes or parts. The old parts go back in stock and the new ones are taken; if a part is missing from stock, nothing changes. Or **Delete** the service. Its parts go back in stock, and if it moved the vehicle's odometer (and you haven't changed the odometer since), the odometer goes back too.

You can also log a service without a kit (Services → Log service), e.g. for a one-off repair. Once the vehicle is chosen (or straight away from a vehicle's page, or while you work on a vehicle), **Start from a kit**, right under the vehicle, lists that vehicle's kits, most urgent first. Each kit ends in its status (*On track*, *Due soon*, *Overdue*; hover it for how far off it is); a kit without a repeat interval is just its name. Picking one opens its form instead and keeps the date, odometer and labour you typed. **Work performed** suggests common maintenance jobs as well as your saved kits and past services.

## Shopping

- **Add to the list** has one **Item** field. Type, and the items in stock that match come up with how much you have; pick one (click it, or the arrow keys and Enter), or keep typing for anything else (the list ends with *Something new: “…”*; Enter with nothing highlighted just closes the list). Typing an item's exact name (or its brand and name, in any case) is that item too, picked or not, so you never get a second entry for something you stock. The end of the box says which it will be: the item's stock badge, or *New* for something new (hover it for the whole sentence). A name two items share (*Oil Filter* from two brands) is neither until you pick one; the box says *2 matches*. Archived items aren't offered; their name adds something new, and **Bought** offers to restore the item. On the whole list the items come in the stock page's groups (*Only KTM 390*, *Shared KTM 390 + KTM 1290*, *General*), so two of one name tell apart by their vehicle. On a vehicle's part of the list, Item offers that vehicle's items (*For KTM 390*, shared ones too) and the General ones, and a name is that vehicle's item.
- **Quantity** is optional: leave it empty when you don't know yet, and Bought asks what you bought. When you give one, Bought and *Add to stock as a new item* start from it (the new item in its unit). Pick the unit next to the quantity, or type it (`4 L`, `500 g`); for an item from your stock the units follow the item. A free-text entry keeps its unit, and adding it to stock later starts the new item in that unit. Adding an item that's already listed adds to its quantity and keeps both notes.
- **Suggestions** list items below their minimum and parts missing for any kit, as long as the list asks for less than that (it then shows how much is already listed). Each says why: *Below the minimum of 2 pcs*, *Short for the Air Filter kit*. Add them one by one (**+**) or all at once (**Add all**, under the suggestions); the reason becomes the entry's note. **Add to shopping list** on an item's page uses the same amount.
- **By vehicle.** On the whole list, each entry and suggestion shows the vehicles it is for as chips (an item's vehicles, and the vehicles whose kits use it; General items and free text have none). The chips above the list show one vehicle's part: what it needs, with the General items and free text. While you work on a vehicle, the list is always its part, and the counts in the sidebar, the tab bar and the dashboard are too. The list says how many entries are left out (**N more for other vehicles**): at the shop, tap the bar's **×** to see everything. **Copy as text** copies what the page shows; the whole list's copy names each entry's vehicles.
- **Bought** on an entry for a stock item records the purchase (amount, price, shop, date, optional best before). If you bought less than listed, the rest stays on the list; otherwise the entry is removed.
- **Bought** on a free-text entry asks what to do. If an item with that name is already in stock (or archived), it is offered first, with its vehicles (from a vehicle's part of the list, only that vehicle's and the General items): **Add to this item** (**Restore and add to it** for an archived item) links the entry to it (restoring it if archived; its text is kept as the note unless it is just the item's name) and opens the usual Bought form. Otherwise:
  - **Add to stock as a new item** opens the new-item form with the name, quantity and note filled in. Add the price and shop under *Purchase*. Saving creates the item with its purchase, removes the entry and goes back to the list you came from (Undo puts the entry back; the item stays).
  - **Just tick it off** removes the entry, for things you don't keep in stock.
- **Copy as text** gives you the list for the shop.

## Removing items and vehicles

- An item that nothing else refers to can be **deleted** (in its page header, or under its **⋯** when the buttons don't fit), together with its own history. Deleting, archiving and removing don't ask first: each can be undone (see below). Only **Delete permanently** and **Empty trash** in the trash ask, in a small window where **Cancel** is ready; Esc or a click outside cancels too.
- An item that has purchases, was used in a service, or is part of a kit gets **Archive** instead. Deleting it would change those records: your spending, the parts of past services, the kit. An archived item disappears from the stock list, dashboard, item pickers and suggestions, and its shopping-list entry moves to the trash (restore the item first to bring it back). Its history, services and kits stay as they were. Archived kit lines are marked so you can edit the kit; they don't count as missing and aren't taken when you log the service. An archived item takes no purchases, uses or adjustments, isn't taken by new services, and its stock and history can't be edited, until you restore it. A past service that used it can still be edited, deleted or restored, as long as its amount of that item stays the same.
- Find archived items with the **Archived** filter on the stock page, and bring one back with **Restore**.
- Kits too: a kit with logged services gets **Archive** (in its page header) instead of Delete, so its services keep it and it keeps its history. An archived kit leaves the active lists, reminders and suggestions; the vehicle page lists it under its kits, marked archived, and on the **Services** page the Service kits card links to them (**N archived kits →**; **← Active kits** goes back). **Restore** on the kit's page brings it back.
- Vehicles work the same way: a vehicle with services or kits gets **Archive** (in its page header) instead of Delete. It leaves the vehicle lists and reminders; its services, kits, labour costs and item tags stay. Its page and its services page no longer offer to log a service, add an item or kit, or update the odometer, and its kits show no reminders. A past service or kit of it that you delete can still be brought back with Undo. Archived vehicles are listed from the link under the vehicles (click **Active vehicles** to switch back); **Restore** brings one back.
- Update a vehicle's odometer in the **Odometer** box on its page; logging a service updates it too. The odometer can't be set lower than a logged service's reading: fix or delete that service first. The vehicle's edit form has no odometer, so saving it never changes the reading. A service's reading also has to fit between the services logged before and after it.

## Undo and the trash

- Every delete keeps a copy: the message after deleting has an **Undo** button. That covers services, kits, stock changes, shopping-list entries (removed or ticked off), items, vehicles and photos. **Bought** on the shopping list can be undone the same way: the purchase is taken back and the entry is put back as it was.
- The **Trash** (in the sidebar, on a phone under **More**, whenever something is there) lists everything deleted, newest first, with **Restore** and **Delete permanently**. A deleted or replaced photo shows its thumbnail there, so several photos of one item can be told apart. Nothing is removed from it until you delete it permanently or click **Empty trash**. Permanently deleting an item or vehicle also deletes what in the trash can't come back without it: for an item, deleted stock changes, services, photos and list entries that used it; for a vehicle, its deleted services and kits.
- Restoring puts things back exactly, with their history and links, and redoes their effect in stock: a restored service takes its parts again and moves the odometer again. If something it belongs to is in the trash too, or archived, restore that first (you're told which). A restored service's reading must still fit between the services logged since. Links to things permanently deleted since (a kit's parts, a service's kit, vehicle tags) are left out, and the message says how many. If it's no longer possible (the stock was used since, a vehicle with the same name exists now, or what it belonged to was permanently deleted) nothing changes and you're told why. Undoing Bought puts back the amount that purchase covered. Restoring a photo swaps it with the item's current photo, which goes to the trash in turn ("The newer photo is in the trash"), so Undo there swaps them back.

## Two tabs at once

If you open a form, then change or delete the same thing in another tab (or someone else does), saving the first form changes nothing. It says *"This item was changed since you opened it (another tab or person). Reload to see the latest, then make your change again."* and keeps what you typed, so you can copy it before you click **Reload**. If it was deleted meanwhile, the message says so and links to the trash. Buttons such as Delete, Archive, Restore (of something archived), Bought and **Remove** on the shopping list behave the same: nothing happens, and you see the latest. Stock changes (+/-, purchases, uses, adjustments) and odometer readings don't count as changes here, so an open edit form still saves after them. A form opened before something was deleted and restored with Undo is out of date too: reload it.

## A save that seems stuck

When you press a Save button (Save item, Save service, …) or Add to stock, Take from stock, …, the button says **Saving…** until the next page opens. On a slow or patchy connection that can take a while. If you're still on the same page after 15 seconds, the button says **Still saving… try again**, and you can press it again. That's safe: every form is saved at most once.

- If the first save didn't get through, pressing it again saves it now.
- If the first save did go through (only its answer got lost), nothing is saved again: no second service, no second purchase, no parts taken twice. You go to what was saved, with the message *"This was saved already (the first Save went through), so it wasn't saved again."* Anything you changed in the form while you waited was **not** applied, so check the record and edit it if something should differ.
- The same goes for a double click, or for going Back to a form you saved and pressing Save again. To add the same thing twice on purpose (two purchases of the same amount), open the form again for the second one.
- If what you saved was deleted since, the message says so and takes you to the trash. After undoing a Bought, its old form is out of date: reload it to buy again.

## Tips

- Distances (odometer readings, kit intervals) are in km unless the app is set up with another unit (`APP_DISTANCE`, e.g. `mi`), which then shows everywhere instead. It is only a label: the numbers stay as you typed them, so changing it doesn't convert anything.
- Decimals take a comma or a point: `1,5` and `1.5` are the same. Don't group thousands: a price of `1.500` or `1,500` is refused, because it could mean 1500 or 1.5, and so is a count in pieces, ml or g written that way (`1,500 ml`). Type `1500` or `1.5`. In L and kg, `1,500` is 1.5. The app prints thousands with a narrow space (`12 000 km`), which reads the same either way, and never shows three decimals: 1.5 L and 1.75 L, but 1 475 ml.
- Amounts take at most two decimals of ml or g, and pieces are whole. A finer amount isn't rounded: the form comes back with what you typed and says why. `0.234 g` gets *"use at most two decimals of g"*, and `1.0004` pieces *"pieces must be a whole number"*. In L and kg you can type more decimals, as long as they come to two of ml or g: `1.2345 kg` is 1234.5 g, but `1.234567 kg` gets *"1.234567 kg is 1234.567 g: use at most two decimals of g"*.
- Photos help to recognise similar parts: open an item → **Add photo**. JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP and HEIC (iPhone) photos work, up to 15 MB and 100 megapixels. Large photos are shrunk in your browser before they upload ("Preparing photo…"), so a phone sends a few hundred KB; the server rotates them upright and resizes them to 1600 px either way. **Replace photo** and **Remove** are on the same spot; both can be undone.
- A USB or Bluetooth barcode scanner "types" into the search box. Put barcodes in the SKU list and scanning finds the item.
- Shortcuts, the same on every page: `/` searches stock, `a` opens Add item, `l` opens Log service. The sidebar shows each key on what it does: `/` on Stock, `a` and `l` on their buttons.
- **Appearance** (the palette button at the top of the sidebar; on a phone, under **More**) sets how the app looks on that device. **Theme**: tap a picture: System follows your system's light or dark mode, or pick Light or Dark. **Accent**: tap a colour (Violet, the default, then Teal, Orchid, Cobalt, Rose, Graphite, Moss and Mauve; a narrow screen shows fewer), or tap the rainbow **Custom** swatch at the end and drag **Colour** round the colour wheel and **Intensity** from grey to full colour; the page changes as you drag. **Reset to default** puts Violet back. Each device remembers its own.
- Fields that suggest as you type (Brand, Category, Location, Shop, Make, Model, Work performed, a kit's Name) keep one spelling of each name: type `ktm` where `KTM` is in use and it is saved as `KTM`. Saving a record leaves a field you didn't change as it was, so to fix an old spelling, retype it (`KTM` over `ktm`). The only record with a spelling can change it; when several share one, each keeps the spelling in use (renaming them all at once is on the [roadmap](roadmap.md)).
- A **?** next to a field explains it: hover over it or tab to it, or tap it to keep it open until you tap elsewhere.
- The *Activity* page lists every stock change across all items, 200 per page (**Older** / **Newer**), filtered by type and year if you like. An item's page shows its first 100 changes (the newest, unless you sorted it otherwise); **Full history →** opens its whole history there, in the same order. The CSV button downloads what the page is filtered on.
- The services list (100 per page) and each vehicle show the labour cost of every service, and the total of all of them in the footer under the list (**Labour in total**). On the services list, **Newer** / **Older** sit in the same footer.
